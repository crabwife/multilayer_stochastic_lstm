## Question and controlled result

Let `q ~ Beta(a,b)` be the propensity to retain an episode for one more step, with the same one-step mean `mu=a/(a+b)` for every policy.

| Policy | When the Beta variable is drawn | Exact probability an episode lasts past T transitions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annealed | A fresh independent q each step | `mu**T` |
| Episodic | One q at the start of each episode; held until a state flip | `B(a+T,b)/B(a,b) ~ Gamma(a+b)/Gamma(a) * T**(-b)` |

The episodic mean dwell is infinite for `b <= 1` and finite for `b > 1`. The default `b=0.6` and `b=1.4` straddle this boundary while retaining `mu=0.95`. The two annealed datasets are **identical in population law** despite their different Beta shapes: redrawing q every step makes each observed state flip with probability `1-mu`. They are independent simulation replicates, useful as a negative control. These formulas concern the **controlled renewal process**, not every trajectory generated by a trained LSTM. Individual trajectories with a fixed continuous forget multiplier `q<1` still lose a particular memory exponentially; the ensemble average has a power-law tail. A separate binary reset mechanism makes the episode durations heavy-tailed, and an alternating two-state renewal with infinite-mean episodes can exhibit weak ergodicity breaking. The independent fresh-draw process is an ergodic short-memory control.

`retention_theory.csv` reports exact and simulated initial-episode survival and the predicted moments. `time_average.csv` reports the variance and quantiles of trajectory time averages at 128, 512 and 2048 steps; `retention_laws.png` plots both. These are **simulation diagnostics**; for finite `b>1`, convergence can still look slow at 2048.

## Matched learned forecast

The model in `src/retention_model.py` is a context encoder plus recurrent decoder with a context-conditioned Beta **forget coefficient**. All three decoder policies use the same layers, width and number of parameters, with a forget distribution fixed by the observed context. The policies are:

- `mean`: use the conditional mean at every decoder update;
- `step`: sample a new Beta forget coefficient at every update;
- `episode`: hold a sampled coefficient until the observed or predicted binary state flips.

The input, output and candidate gates, encoder and Bernoulli readout are identical. This is a controlled experimental recurrent model, **not an implementation of StoxLSTM** (the Gaussian-latent xLSTM). Only the forecast decoder's retention schedule changes. The one-step gate marginal is matched at the beginning of an episode; survival selection means the conditional distribution among still-active episodes changes with age, as intended.

Training maximizes a particle approximation to the **joint 16-step teacher-forced path likelihood** using trajectory-disjoint training examples. Selection uses only validation path likelihood. The locked test generates 128-step autoregressive paths, scored at both 32 and 128 steps. Compared with the earlier one-step objective, this trains for joint dependence, though 16-step training does not guarantee calibration at 128. Model checkpoints cache by exact settings and data signature.

`validation.csv` gives fit epochs, path likelihood, parameter counts and cost. `summary.csv` and `trajectory_scores.csv` give frequency CRPS (lower is better), 90% interval coverage, variance of predicted time averages and observed means. `paired_contrasts.csv` compares separately fitted policies on the same independent test trajectories, averaging training replicates within each trajectory before bootstrapping.

The more direct architectural test is the **inference-time crossover**: each saved checkpoint is rolled out under all three policies with the **same weights, context-conditioned Beta parameters, test trajectories and Bernoulli emission random stream**. `schedule_interventions.csv` and `schedule_intervention_summary.csv` include both `trained_policy` and `policy` (the policy used at inference). `paired_interventions.csv` reports within-checkpoint differences. An intervention can create a train–test policy mismatch, so read it alongside separately trained comparisons. `learned_retention.csv` records the mean gate value, median concentration, and median learned second Beta shape: verify that the model actually learned a long-retention regime. All intervals condition on two seeds and the simulated dataset.

**Decision rule:** the episodic policy should improve long-horizon time-average CRPS on the heavy-tailed (`b=0.6`) process, with calibration and variance closer to the simulated process, without a comparable advantage on annealed control. A result on the `b=1.4` case should be interpreted as slow finite-horizon memory unless time-average variance is shown to persist as the horizon grows. Report negative results too. The three policies have the same parameter count, but a future publication should add more training/data seeds, a direct StoxLSTM baseline only if claiming an extension of it, and longer held-out horizons. The Beta-gate idea itself has prior work; the tested hypothesis here is **temporal persistence of the forget draw**.
